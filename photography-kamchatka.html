Me and Dasha – the head of our crew – on a watershed of Itchayvayam Ural-Alaskan type complex. It was a three-day ambitious route – we had to sample the main lithologies of the complex and pan several streams. 2016, Koryak Highlands.

All-terrain dog

The main means of transportation in our 2015 mapping fieldwork was an all-terrain vehicle. A huge noisy machine that by its outlook is more suitable for the military. It could cross the tundra and was much cheaper compared to helicopter.

Bear on Shiveluch

We were climbing the western remnant of the old Shiveluch edifice when a pair of brown bears threw themselves towards us. It was scary! Though, turned out that the bears – a mother and a quite grown-up cub – were even more scared. By accident, we pushed them towards the cliff, and they just did not know where to go! On this photo you can see the heels of a cub – he/she was running for their life!

Tolbachik lava cave

One of the multiple lava caves formed during 2012–2013 Tolbachik fissure eruption. During the eruption, these caves were the channels through which lava flowed. Where the roof of the channels collapsed, skylights formed. The entrance into this cave was one of these skylights. In 2018, when the photo was taken, the temperature in some caves still exceeded 80ºC (though in this one it was ≈30ºC – still much warmer than +5ºC outside).

Field portrait

A portrait of my happy self, Tumrok range. This was a two-week fieldwork campaign that I organized using RFBR grant money. We studied late Cretaceous arc picrites and related plutonic complex. In the background, behind the clouds, there are volcanoes of Klyuchevskaya group – Udina, Tolbachik, Zimina. August 2021.
