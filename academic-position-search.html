PhDs and academic positions

The short version of what I tell students who ask.

Start here

Pick the research area first

  1. 1. Choose a topic, not a university

    What would you happily read about for four years? Ore deposits, volcanoes, isotopes, whatever it is.

  2. 2. See who publishes on it

    Search Google Scholar for the last five years of papers. The names that keep coming up are your potential supervisors.

  3. 3. Follow them

    Set Google Scholar alerts on those people and a few keywords. Check their group pages: new projects often show up there first.

  4. 4. Write to them

    A short email: who you are, what interests you in their work, CV attached. Some projects are shaped around a good applicant.

Using them

How to search

  • Search broad words. Petrology, geochemistry, mineral deposits, critical metals. The same project can be advertised under very different titles.
  • Check once a week. Use alerts or RSS where the board has them. Good posts can close within a few weeks.
  • Know the season. UK funded PhDs mostly open in autumn and close around January. In the Netherlands, Germany and Scandinavia a PhD is a paid job, advertised all year. In the US you apply to a graduate programme by December–January, ideally after emailing a supervisor.
  • Read the funding line. "Fully funded" should mean fees plus a stipend. Check whether it covers international fees: some UK studentships pay home fees only.
  • Boards are not everything. Posts also go on university vacancy pages, society newsletters, mailing lists and group websites.
